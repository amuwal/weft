52 weeks of people

One quiet question to ask someone you love — one for each week of the year. Not an interview, not a worksheet. Just a small prompt for the kind of conversation that doesn't happen on its own.

Read one on a Sunday. Pick a person. Ask it the next time you talk — over coffee, on the phone, in a text. Write down what they say, if you want to remember it.

How to use it

There is no right order and no schedule to keep. Some weeks you'll ask no one, and that's fine. The questions move loosely with the seasons — beginnings in winter, opening up in spring, ease in summer, gathering in toward the end of the year — but a good question keeps in any month. Skip the ones that don't fit. Re-ask the ones that do.

Winter

Weeks 1–13 · beginnings

The quiet part of the year. Questions about what's starting, what someone is carrying, and what they're hoping the months ahead will hold.

  1. What's something small you're looking forward to this month?
  2. Is there anything you've been carrying on your own lately?
  3. What did you change your mind about this past year?
  4. Who's been good to you recently — and have you told them?
  5. What's a part of your ordinary day that you actually like?
  6. Is there a decision you keep putting off?
  7. What's been on your mind on the drive home lately?
  8. What did your family always do this time of year?
  9. What would a genuinely good month look like for you?
  10. Is there something you wish people understood about you?
  11. What's the last thing that made you cry — happy or sad?
  12. Who from your past do you find yourself thinking about?
  13. What feels heavier right now than it probably should?

Spring

Weeks 14–26 · opening up

Questions that let a little more light in — about hopes, change, the people who shaped someone, and the things they've quietly wanted.

  1. What do you want this year that you didn't a year ago?
  2. When did you last feel proud of yourself, and for what?
  3. Who taught you something you still carry?
  4. What's a small risk you've been thinking about taking?
  5. What's a place you'd go back to if you could?
  6. What were you like as a kid — and what's left of that?
  7. Is there something you've finally forgiven yourself for?
  8. What's a friendship you wish you'd kept?
  9. What's been making you laugh lately?
  10. What do you do when no one's asking anything of you?
  11. What's a kindness someone did for you that you've never forgotten?
  12. What's changed about how you see your parents?
  13. What would you say to yourself five years ago?

Summer

Weeks 27–39 · ease

Lighter on purpose — joy, play, the everyday textures of a life. The questions you can ask at a picnic table without it getting heavy.

  1. What's the best day you've had this year so far?
  2. What's a small thing that reliably makes you happy?
  3. What are you reading, watching, or listening to lately?
  4. Where do you feel most like yourself?
  5. What's a meal that means something to you?
  6. What's something you're surprisingly good at?
  7. What did you want to be when you were ten?
  8. What's a trip — real or imagined — you keep daydreaming about?
  9. Who makes you feel completely at ease?
  10. What's a habit you've picked up that you're glad about?
  11. What's a song that takes you somewhere?
  12. What's the most fun you've had lately that cost nothing?
  13. What's something you'd love to learn just for the joy of it?

Autumn

Weeks 40–52 · gathering in

The turn toward home and reflection. Questions about what someone is grateful for, what they're letting go of, and where they'd like to be held a little closer.

  1. What are you quietly grateful for right now?
  2. What's something you'd like to do more of before the year ends?
  3. Who do you wish you saw more often?
  4. What's a worry that turned out to be smaller than you feared?
  5. What are you ready to let go of?
  6. When did you last feel truly understood by someone?
  7. What does it look like, for you, when someone gets it right?
  8. What's a tradition you'd like to keep — or start?
  9. What's the kindest thing you did for someone this year?
  10. What do you hope is different by this time next year?
  11. Is there anything you've been meaning to say to me?
  12. What's a memory of us you find yourself coming back to?
  13. Is there a way I could be a better friend to you?
